# Counterexamples topple the bound; Z/4 stays open

As of 23 November 2026, neither question is settled in the literature: nobody has proved or refuted that the toric ideal of the general group-based model for G = Z/4 on the claw tree K_{1,n} is generated in degree ≤ 4 for all n, and the constancy conjecture φ(G, n+1) = max(2, φ(G, n)) is a theorem only for Z/2, Z/3 and Z/2×Z/2. For Z/4 the two questions are the same question: the only published values are φ(Z/4, 3) = φ(Z/4, 4) = 4 (Sturmfels–Sullivant, 4ti2, 2004), φ is non-decreasing in n, and so "degree ≤ 4 for all n" is exactly constancy for Z/4, the smallest group for which constancy is open. Michałek–Ventura's 2017 finiteness theorem shows φ(Z/4, n) is eventually constant, but the threshold is not stated in the paper, and unwinding the proof gives a number of order 10^4503 leaves, so it reduces nothing to a feasible computation. The surrounding landscape changed in September 2026: Pochuev's explicit pair of 18×3 tables over Z/17 refutes the Sturmfels–Sullivant bound φ(G) ≤ |G| and its three-leaf form, and computations made for this report (verified in session, unpublished) show the same mechanism already breaks the bound for Z/12 (degree 13) and Z/13 (degree 17), with the Z/12 and Z/17 cases and the monotonicity lemma machine-checked in Lean 4. None of this touches constancy or Z/4 directly, but it removes the general bound as supporting evidence and shows that extrapolation from small cases is unsafe in this subject. A systematic citation and arXiv sweep found no work since 2017 that computes φ(Z/4, 5), bounds φ(Z/4), or addresses constancy; the most valuable next step is the still-missing five-leaf computation for Z/4, followed by a computer-assisted Z/4 analogue of the Michałek–Ventura Kimura-3 proof.

## Two indexing conventions hide one combinatorial problem about tables

**The objects.** In a group-based model the transition matrices depend only on differences of states in a finite abelian group G; the discrete Fourier transform (Evans–Speed, Székely–Steel–Erdős) turns the parametrisation into a monomial map, so the ideal of phylogenetic invariants in Fourier coordinates is toric ([Sturmfels–Sullivant](https://arxiv.org/abs/q-bio/0402015)). For the claw tree and the *general* model (identity labelling, all parameters distinct), Sturmfels–Sullivant define I_{G,n} as the kernel of q_{g_1,…,g_n} ↦ a^(1)_{g_1}···a^(n)_{g_n}·a^(n+1)_{g_1+···+g_n} and set "φ(G, n) … the largest degree of any minimal generator of the toric ideal I_{G,n}" and φ(G) := max_n φ(G, n) ([Sturmfels–Sullivant](https://arxiv.org/abs/q-bio/0402015)). The later literature uses *flows*: a flow is an n-tuple of elements of G summing to 0, the flows form a group isomorphic to G^{n−1}, each flow gives a 0/1 vertex Σ_i e_(i,g_i) of the polytope P_{G,n} ⊂ R^{n|G|}, and "φ(G, n) [is] the maximal degree of a generator in a minimal generating set of I(X(G, K_{1,n}))", with φ(G) = sup_n φ(G, n) ([Michałek, survey §11](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03125)). Michałek 2013 takes the supremum over all trees instead ([Michałek 2013, Def. 4.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.0930)); the two definitions agree because Sturmfels–Sullivant's local-structure theorem generates the ideal of any tree from the lifted claw-tree generators at each interior vertex plus quadrics for each interior edge, giving φ(G, T) ≤ max(φ(G, T_1), φ(G, T_2)) for a tree glued from T_1 and T_2 ([Sturmfels–Sullivant](https://arxiv.org/abs/q-bio/0402015); [Michałek 2013, Prop. 4.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.0930)). All difficulty therefore lives in claw trees.

**The n trap.** Sturmfels–Sullivant and Chifman–Petrović index by the number of free Fourier indices (the tree has n+1 edges), while Donten-Bury–Michałek, Michałek, Michałek–Ventura, the survey and Dinu–Vodička index by the total number of leaves. The conversion, which no paper states explicitly, is **φ_SS(G, n) = φ_flow(G, n+1)**: the tripod is n = 2 in the 2005 table and n = 3 everywhere else, and the zero ideal for Z/2 sits at n_SS = 2, i.e. three leaves ([Sturmfels–Sullivant](https://arxiv.org/abs/q-bio/0402015); [Donten-Bury–Michałek](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3236)). A second trap is numbering: the journal version of Sturmfels–Sullivant is shifted by +2 relative to arXiv v1, so the bound conjecture is "Conjecture 27" on arXiv and "Conjecture 29" in every later citation, the Z/2×Z/2 conjecture is 28/30, and the tree-reduction theorem cited as "[SS05, Theorem 26]" is arXiv Theorem 24 (the notes infer this shift from three matching cross-citations; the journal PDF was not inspected). Mic17 further writes ψ(n, G) with arguments reversed ([Michałek 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)). This report uses the leaf convention throughout.

**The dictionary.** A degree-d binomial is a pair of d×n tables T_0, T_1 whose rows are flows; it lies in the ideal exactly when the tables are *compatible*, meaning each column of T_0 equals the corresponding column of T_1 as a multiset. The ideal is generated in degree ≤ d if and only if any two compatible tables are connected by moves that each replace at most d rows by a compatible set of rows ([Michałek, survey §11](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03125); [Michałek–Ventura 2019, §2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)). Three symmetry groups act: S_n on columns, the group of flows by translation of every row, and Aut(G) entrywise ([Michałek–Ventura 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)). Structurally, P_{G,n} has |G|^{n−1} vertices, all its lattice points are vertices, and the lattice its vertices generate is cut out by equal block sums and a G-valued weighted-sum condition ([Donten-Bury–Michałek, Thm 2.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3236); [Dinu–Vodička, Thm 2.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16812)); the index is stated as 2, 3, 4 for Z/2, Z/3, Z/2×Z/2 and is |G| in general by the same formula (the general statement is an inference in the notes, not a quoted theorem).

**The conjectures, verbatim.** Sturmfels–Sullivant: "For any finite abelian group G and any positive integer n we have φ(G, n) ≤ |G|" and "The phylogenetic complexity φ(G) of the group G = Z2 × Z2 is four" ([Sturmfels–Sullivant](https://arxiv.org/abs/q-bio/0402015)). Michałek 2013, Conjecture 9.3: "The function φ(G, ·) is constant", presented as a consequence of Conjecture 9.2, that X(K_{1,n}, G) is the scheme-theoretic intersection of the varieties of its prolongations with smaller valency (originally Donten-Bury–Michałek Conjecture 3.6) ([Michałek 2013, §9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.0930); [Donten-Bury–Michałek](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3236)). Michałek–Ventura 2017, Conjecture 4.3: "We have φ(G, n + 1) = max(2, φ(G, n))", with the remark, credited to Sullivant, "that this is equivalent to φ(G, ·) being constant, apart from the case when G = Z2 and n = 3, when the associated variety is the whole projective space"; the same paper states Conjecture 4.2, φ(G, 3) ≤ |G| ([Michałek–Ventura 2017, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07263)). The max(2, ·) is thus a repair of the literally false 2013 wording, needed only because φ(Z/2, 3) = 0 and φ(Z/2, 4) = 2.

The Z/3 five-leaf value follows by sandwiching, and Michałek 2017 reads Donten-Bury–Michałek as covering all n < 7 ([Michałek 2017, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)). **No value of φ(Z/4, n) for n ≥ 5 appears anywhere**, nor any value for Z/5 beyond four leaves, for Z/6, Z/7 or the order-8 groups beyond three leaves, or an exact value for any group of order ≥ 9; Donten-Bury–Michałek report that for Z/9 they could not even check normality ([Donten-Bury–Michałek](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3236)). The Z/4 three- and four-leaf counts were reproduced in session with 4ti2 1.6.10 (0.05 s and 11 s; unpublished). **Monotonicity is folklore with a two-line proof.** Michałek–Ventura prove only φ(G, n) ≤ φ(G, n−1) for large n and then write "This implies that they are equal"; Michałek 2017 does the same; no source read states the reverse inequality as a lemma ([Michałek–Ventura 2017, Thm 3.12](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07263); [Michałek 2017, §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)). The proof is that appending a zero column to two compatible tables keeps them compatible, every move preserves column multisets and hence keeps that column zero, and deleting it returns a chain of moves of the same sizes; geometrically P_{G,n} is the face of P_{G,n+1} where the last leaf carries 0, as Michałek–Ventura use for Z/2×Z/2 at six leaves ([Michałek–Ventura 2019, Lemma 3.26](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)). **The lemma φ(G, n) ≤ φ(G, n+1) has been machine-checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib (verified in session, unpublished).** Three consequences follow. First, constancy is equivalent to the single family of inequalities φ(G, n+1) ≤ max(2, φ(G, n)), and to φ(G) = max(2, φ(G, 3)). Second, it is already a theorem for Z/2, Z/3 and Z/2×Z/2, because their upper bounds meet the tripod values. Third, since φ(Z/4, 3) = 4, **question (a) and question (b) for G = Z/4 are literally the same statement**, φ(Z/4, n) = 4 for all n ≥ 3; unconditionally φ(Z/4) ≥ 4, φ(Z/5) ≥ 4, φ(Z/7) ≥ 6, and constancy would make these equalities.

## The three solved cases share a skeleton but not their engines

**Z/2.** Sturmfels–Sullivant's proof is a short minimal-counterexample argument: translate so one row of T is zero, choose a counterexample minimising (degree m, number k of disagreements), and observe that in the two rightmost columns any pair 00 in T′ or 11 in T allows a swap that lowers m, forcing the two column sums to be ≥ m+1 in T′ and ≤ m−1 in T, contradicting compatibility ([Sturmfels–Sullivant](https://arxiv.org/abs/q-bio/0402015)). Chifman–Petrović add an explicit recursive generating set and a quadratic lexicographic Gröbner basis, citing Sturmfels–Sullivant for degree-2 generation and remarking that the Z/2 case "has been solved … using a technique that does not generalize"; one S-pair case is only sketched ("A very technical argument shows…") ([Chifman–Petrović](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0702368)). The translation normalisation and the minimal-counterexample frame transfer to any group. The closing count does not: a Z/2 column is one statistic and the only swap with a zero row is 00 ↔ 11, whereas a Z/4 column multiset has three independent counts and minimal generators of degree 3 and 4 already exist on the tripod.

**Z/3.** Michałek's proof is a triple induction: on n (base n < 7 by computer), on degree d > 3, and on the number of agreements between a chosen row f_1 and the zero row. Either a short move increases agreement, after which the common row is deleted and one inducts on d, or the configuration is so rigid that all rows agree with a template h off a few columns except at one index (Propositions 4.4, 4.21), in which case two columns i, j carry equal multisets of pairs in both tables (Lemma 4.15), are summed to give tables with n−1 columns, and the moves are lifted back ([Michałek 2017, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)). The engine is Lemma 3.1, stated only for Z_p and proved by the Combinatorial Nullstellensatz: two flows differing on p−1 indices can be exchanged on any prescribed further set of indices. Cubic moves appear only at isolated points, mostly in the Hamming-distance-2 case. The author's own verdict: "we believe that it is impossible to follow this proof without a pen and a sheet of paper. The author would much appreciate an approach that would be significantly simpler" ([Michałek 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)).

**Z/2×Z/2.** Michałek–Ventura's 32-page paper is "devoted solely to the proof of the main theorem". The architecture is nested induction: n ≤ 5 by computer, with n = 5 needing 4ti2 on a 32-core server with 1024 GB RAM; n = 6 by a hybrid, because direct computation "would run for several years … and a memory of 1Tb RAM would not be sufficient", so Normaliz verifies normality of the 18-dimensional polytope, Cohen–Macaulayness gives a regularity bound (Markov degree ≤ 16, ≤ 14 on two faces), hand counting excludes degrees 5 to 16, and 4ti2 handles three codimension-3 faces; n ≥ 7 by hand, inducting on degree d ≥ 5 and on the Hamming distance k between a closest pair of rows ([Michałek–Ventura 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)). Distances ≥ 3 are reduced to 2 using counting functions (four classes of 4-letter disagreement strings, then the string αβγ); distance 2, string αα, splits into ten cases, seven reduced to a three-case "main case" in which "bad pairs" (two nonzero entries in columns n−1, n) are eliminated with moves of degree ≤ 4 and the columns merged by Theorem 3.4. The proof uses Aut(G) ≅ S_3 acting transitively on nonzero elements, x + x = 0, α + β + γ = 0, and the three quotients to Z/2 for parity counts, on nearly every page ([Michałek–Ventura 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)). Michałek describes the result as reached "after ten years of fight — maybe even war" ([Michałek's conjectures page](https://www.math.uni-konstanz.de/~michalek/conjectures.html)). Donten-Bury's scheme-theoretic Z/3 theorem is a different and easier kind of statement: saturation permits appending zero rows, after which every flow splits into minimal zero-sum "pairs" and "triples" and a finite case analysis suffices ([Donten-Bury](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4169)).

**What transfers to Z/4.** The group-independent layer transfers verbatim: the table/move dictionary, the three symmetry normalisations, counting functions and double counting, the induction skeleton on (n, d, Hamming distance), the fact that Hamming distance 1 is impossible, and column merging, which needs only equal multisets of pairs in the two merged columns ([Michałek–Ventura 2019, Thm 3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584); [Michałek–Ventura 2017, Lemma 3.3 and Thm 3.12](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07263)). The base cases have the same size as for Kimura-3, 4^{n−1} variables. What does not transfer is every local exchange lemma, for reasons the notes derive by elementary hand computation (their assessment, not statements from the papers). Lemma 3.1 fails for Z/4: if all differences equal 2 the subset sums stay in {0, 2}, so rows congruent mod 2 form a new obstruction class; the correct analogue requires three disagreements with at least one odd. Aut(Z/4) = {±1} has two orbits on nonzero elements instead of one, giving 9 classes of 4-letter disagreement strings instead of 4 and two inequivalent distance-2 roots, (1,3) and (2,2), instead of one. The Davenport constant is 4 rather than 3, so minimal zero-sum patterns such as 1111 contain no zero-sum pair or triple. The Difference and Standard Lemmas of the Kimura-3 proof rest on x = −x and α+β+γ = 0, both false in Z/4. The notes' candid estimate is "Kimura-3 times 2 to 4" in case volume with at least three genuinely new lemmas, a judgement from counting strings and symmetries rather than a measurement. No paper read contains an attempted Z/4 proof, singles out Z/4 as the next case, or states a Z/4-specific obstruction.

## Finiteness theorems guarantee stabilisation only beyond 10^4503 leaves

Four levels of finiteness are known for general G, from coarse to fine. On the open torus orbit, Casanellas–Fernández-Sánchez–Michałek show the variety is a complete intersection of explicit binomials of degree ≤ max(3, a_i) ≤ |G| for G = ∏ Z_{a_i}, and that the claw-tree variety is there the scheme-theoretic intersection of two smaller-valency tree varieties; this is Conjecture 9.2 restricted to the torus, a statement about the lattice of relations that bounds nothing about ideal generators ([Casanellas–Fernández-Sánchez–Michałek](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.6945)). Set-theoretically, Draisma–Eggermont give a tree-independent bound D(B, G) by Noetherianity up to symmetry, and say "our proofs are non-constructive" ([Draisma–Eggermont](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.1282)). For the projective scheme, Michałek 2013 gives a constructive tree-independent bound (and degree 4 for Z/2×Z/2), via Lemma 7.2, which after adding copies of the zero flow reduces any relation by quadrics to flows of support at most the Davenport constant, and Lemma 7.3, which merges two coordinates; the constant N there "can be explicitly computed" but is not ([Michałek 2013, §7](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.0930)). Ideal-theoretically, Michałek–Ventura's Theorem 3.12 states "For any finite abelian group G, the phylogenetic complexity φ(G) of G is finite", by proving φ(G, n) ≤ φ(G, n−1) for n > N: quadratic moves make two adjacent columns agree row-by-row (Lemmas 3.10–3.11, Ramsey-type arguments on "frequent" elements with threshold F(G) > |G|² + 3|G|), the columns are summed, the (n−1)-column relation is generated in degree φ(G, n−1), and the moves lift ([Michałek–Ventura 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07263)). Michałek 2017 had done this for Z_p ([Michałek 2017, Thm 1.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)).

The paper's only quantification is "Fix N ≫ |G|". The notes unwound the recursion in Lemma 3.9 at face value: with g = |G|, B = g(F(G)+1) and L = B + g − 2, the threshold is K(B, g, 3) = 2·3^L·g^{L(L−1)/2} and N ≈ g·K. **For Z/4 this gives N ≈ 2·3^122·4^7382 ≈ 10^4503**, about 10^903 for order 3 and 10^15884 for order 5. This figure is the notes' own derivation, taken from lemmas whose side conditions are loosely specified in the text, and has not been checked against any published source; a rigorous effective version might need a larger constant. Its message is robust: the proof is effective in principle (the only non-constructive step, finiteness of φ(G, N) by the Hilbert basis theorem, can be replaced by Sturmfels' general toric degree bound), but it controls φ only beyond an astronomically large N and says nothing about whether φ changes between n = 3 and n = N. Tightening constants cannot help, because the growth comes from a nested subdivision over roughly |G|³ steps.

Donten-Bury–Michałek's Proposition 3.9, "Conjecture 3.6 holds for n large enough if and only if the function φ is bounded", has a far smaller threshold: a binomial of degree ≤ m lies in the ideal of some two-inner-vertex prolongation once n > |G|^m + 1 ([Donten-Bury–Michałek](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3236)). Combined with finiteness this yields the intersection conjecture for all n > |G|^{φ(G)} + 1 (a combination neither paper states), and shows a new minimal generator of degree d must first appear at some n ≤ 4^d + 1 for Z/4. It works degree by degree and cannot bound d, so it also fails to reduce the problem to a finite check. The implication from Conjecture 9.2 to constancy exists only as a sketch in Donten-Bury–Michałek ("the ideal of X(K_{n,1}) is just the sum of ideals of trees with smaller valency"), and requires the affine (ideal-equality) reading of "scheme-theoretic" ([Donten-Bury–Michałek, §3.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3236)). Toric fibre products give one inclusion of that conjecture only: K_{1,n+1} is not a fibre product of smaller claws, and X(K_{1,n+1}) merely sits inside the variety of the glued tree ([Sullivant](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0602052); [Michałek 2013, §9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.0930)).

## A face with d+2 vertices breaks the bound at Z/17, Z/13 and Z/12

**The Z/17 example.** Pochuev's note, dated 8 September 2026 and distributed through figshare/Zenodo and GitHub rather than arXiv, exhibits two 18×3 tables A and B of zero-sum rows over Z/17 with equal column multisets and no common row, and proves they are the only two tables with those column multisets ([Pochuev, GitHub](https://github.com/Grisha-Pochuev/phylogenetic-complexity-z17-counterexample); [figshare record](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.33471694)). The column supports have sizes 7, 9, 7; exactly 22 flows lie in their product; the 23×22 incidence matrix M of (column, value) against row types has rank 21, with kernel spanned over Q by β − α, the difference of the two multiplicity vectors; and because one coordinate of β − α is 1 and another forces 1 − t ≥ 0, the only non-negative integer solutions are α and β. A move on fewer than 18 rows keeps some row of A, so its result is a table in the fibre sharing a row with A, hence A itself: the tables cannot be connected by smaller moves in any number of steps, and **φ(Z/17, 3) ≥ 18 > 17**. Appending zero columns gives φ(Z/17, n) ≥ 18 for all n ≥ 3. The note makes no minimality claim, does not assert φ(Z/17) = 18, does not describe how the example was found beyond "AI assistance", and calls itself a "working manuscript for independent verification" ([Pochuev, GitHub](https://github.com/Grisha-Pochuev/phylogenetic-complexity-z17-counterexample)). Michałek's conjectures page records: "Grisha Pochuev recently produced a counterexample for ℤ/17ℤ, found with ChatGPT Astra", in payment of a standing dinner-for-a-counterexample offer ([Michałek's conjectures page](https://www.math.uni-konstanz.de/~michalek/conjectures.html)). Two independent scripts written for the notes re-derived every stated invariant from the data file alone, and **the Z/17 case has additionally been machine-checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib (verified in session, unpublished)**.

**What it refutes.** The example kills Sturmfels–Sullivant's Conjecture 27/29, Donten-Bury–Michałek's Conjecture 3.1, the survey's Conjecture 11.6, and, being a three-leaf example, Michałek–Ventura's Conjecture 4.2. Pochuev's Proposition 4 shows that setting all variables outside the 22 row types to zero maps the whole ideal onto the principal ideal (X^α − X^β); his Corollary 5 then observes that the point with coordinate 1 on the 11 row types of A and 0 elsewhere is a zero of every element of degree ≤ 17 but not of the degree-18 binomial, so equations of degree ≤ 17 fail to cut out the variety even set-theoretically, at a boundary point ([Pochuev, GitHub](https://github.com/Grisha-Pochuev/phylogenetic-complexity-z17-counterexample)). That also refutes Michałek's Conjecture 9.1 (degree |G| suffices for the projective scheme) for Z/17 on the tripod; this consequence is drawn here and in the notes, not claimed in Pochuev's manuscript. It is consistent with the torus-orbit theorem, where degree ≤ |G| does suffice: the failure lives on coordinate hyperplanes. It leaves untouched constancy, the intersection conjectures, finiteness for non-abelian groups, and Michałek's Conjecture 9.4 of a polynomial bound on φ(G, K_{1,3}).

**The mechanism.** A fibre consisting of two monomials with disjoint supports is exactly the fibre of an *indispensable* binomial, one that appears up to sign in every binomial generating set ([Charalambous–Katsabekis–Thoma, Cor. 2.10](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0607249)). In the Z/17 example the supports cut out a face of the tripod polytope with 22 vertices and dimension 20, a face with d+2 vertices, whose toric ideal is principal, generated by its unique circuit; the degree of the generator is the sum of the positive entries of the primitive kernel vector. The "determinant 17" certificate in the note has no arithmetic significance: because the vertex lattice has index |G|, every maximal minor of such a face matrix is divisible by |G|, and the notes compute the Smith normal form diag(1^20, 17, 0) and all 22 column-deleted minors as 17·(β − α)_k (session computation, unpublished). Nothing ties the degree to |G| + 1.

**Smaller groups violate the bound too (verified in session, unpublished).** A short random search over support triples found rank-one-kernel faces of excess degree for smaller groups. For **Z/12, degree 13** (supports of sizes 5, 7, 7; 18 flows; nullity 1):

A = (0,11,1), (4,2,6)×2, (4,4,4)×2, (6,5,1), (6,11,7), (7,6,11)×2, (7,9,8)×2, (10,3,11), (10,9,5);
B = (0,5,7), (4,3,5), (4,9,11)×3, (6,2,4)×2, (7,4,1)×2, (7,11,6)×2, (10,6,8)×2.

For **Z/13, degree 17** (supports 6, 6, 8; 19 flows; nullity 1):

A = (1,7,5), (1,12,0), (2,3,8)×2, (2,5,6), (4,9,0)×2, (5,7,1)×2, (6,9,11), (6,10,10)×4, (7,5,1), (7,12,7)×2;
B = (1,5,7)×2, (2,10,1)×3, (4,3,6), (4,12,10), (5,3,5), (5,10,11), (6,7,0)×3, (6,12,8)×2, (7,9,10)×3.

Hence **φ(Z/12, 3) ≥ 13 and φ(Z/13, 3) ≥ 17**. Both were found in the notes' session and then **independently re-verified in the coordinating session by a separate exhaustive fibre enumeration (fibre of exactly two tables, no common row); the Z/12 case is machine-checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib as theorem `not_generatedInDegree_zmod12`, depending only on the axioms propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound.** None of this is published or externally reviewed. The same search produced a second, different degree-18 example for Z/17 and a degree-20 example for Z/19. So 17 is not the smallest violating order, and the failure is not confined to primes or odd orders. Since every abelian group of order ≤ 8 has φ(G, 3) ≤ |G| by the published exact values, **the smallest order violating the three-leaf bound is 9, 10, 11 or 12**. For Z/9 an exhaustive enumeration of 10.1 million support triples caps the rank-one-kernel mechanism at degree 8, so that mechanism provably cannot break the bound there; for Z/3×Z/3, Z/10 and Z/11 searches reached only 8, 10 and 10. These searches cannot certify φ ≤ |G|, because top-degree generators need not come from rank-one-kernel faces: a session fibre analysis of 4ti2 Markov bases shows the maximal generators for Z/3, Z/5 and Z/7 live in three-element fibres, while for Z/4, Z/2×Z/2 and Z/6 they are indispensable. For every group with an element of order |G|/2 a squarefree circuit of degree exactly |G| exists on the tripod (supports {0, g}, G, G), so where the bound holds for even order it is attained by this same mechanism.

**Normality sorts the violators but proves nothing about degrees.** Dinu–Vodička's Theorem 5.1 states that the tripod polytope P_{G,3} "is normal if and only if G ∈ {Z2, Z3, Z2 × Z2, Z4, Z5, Z7}", extends this to trivalent trees, reports a computer check that the 4-claw polytopes for Z/4, Z/5, Z/7 are normal, and conjectures normality for all trees for exactly these six groups ([Dinu–Vodička](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05301)). Normality is known for every tree for Z/2, Z/3 and Z/2×Z/2 ([Vodička](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.11057); [Dinu–Vodička](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16812)); earlier, Donten-Bury–Michałek had found Z/6, Z/8, Z/4×Z/2, (Z/2)^3 non-normal on the tripod and shown non-normality passes to overgroups ([Donten-Bury–Michałek](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3236)). Normality and the degree bound are logically independent: Z/6 and the order-8 groups are non-normal yet satisfy φ(G, 3) = |G|. Every group now known to violate the bound is non-normal, and the notes give an elementary link (their argument, unpublished): a face of a normal polytope is normal, and a hypersurface x^{u+} − x^{u−} is normal only if one side is squarefree, so in a normal claw polytope every rank-one-kernel circuit has a squarefree side. All violating examples have multiplicities ≥ 2 on both sides and are therefore themselves certificates of non-normality.

**Bearing on Z/4.** The mechanism is starved for Z/4. An exhaustive session enumeration of all support triples on three leaves finds rank-one-kernel circuits of degree 3 or 4 only; on four leaves all 96 quartic and 256 cubic generators are indispensable and none of higher degree exists; heuristic searches on 4, 5 and 6 leaves (millions of evaluations each) found nothing above degree 4; and normality, proved for the tripod and computer-checked for the 4-claw, excludes the high-multiplicity circuits behind all known violations wherever it holds. These searches cover only the rank-one-kernel sub-mechanism for n ≥ 5 and are unpublished. The counterexamples therefore leave the Z/4 case where it was, with one methodological caveat: every abelian group of order ≤ 8 obeys the bound, and that was a poor guide to order 12, so the analogous extrapolation in the number of leaves deserves no trust without proof.

## Nothing since 2017 addresses Z/4 or constancy

A sweep on 22–23 November 2026 of all works citing the seed papers since 2018 (84 distinct works via OpenAlex across twelve seed records, with Crossref counts consistent) and a battery of arXiv API queries found **no paper that proves anything about generators for Z/4 or any cyclic group of order ≥ 4 on claw trees, computes a new value φ(G, n), or addresses constancy in n**. The only post-2017 theorem on phylogenetic complexity is φ(Z/2×Z/2) = 4 ([Michałek–Ventura 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)). The adjacent work is on normality and the Gorenstein property ([Vodička](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.11057); [Dinu–Vodička](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05301)), volumes and degrees of P_{G,n} for Z/2, Z/2×Z/2, Z/3 ([Dinu–Vodička](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16812)), networks, maximum-likelihood degrees and software. The single 2026 item naming Z/4 on a claw tree is Marín Muñoz's note, which shows the tripod semigroup is a complete intersection if and only if |G| ≤ 3 and still describes φ(G) ≤ |G| as a conjecture six days before Pochuev's note ([Marín Muñoz](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02630)). The Z/17 note has zero recorded citations and no arXiv follow-up was found; apart from Michałek's web page there is no published reaction. No Gröbner basis, squarefree initial ideal or unimodular triangulation for Z/4 on any claw tree was located.

The limits of this negative finding matter. Google Scholar, MathSciNet, zbMATH and Semantic Scholar were not queried; about half the citing works were read at abstract level only; arXiv full text is not searchable through the API, and its date-range filter behaved unreliably; MathOverflow and informal channels were checked by one web search; unpublished work by the specialists is unknowable. Within those limits, "complete literature" means: problem (a) and constancy for every group other than the three solved ones are open and untouched.

General stabilisation machinery does not apply off the shelf. Equivariant Noetherianity for toric maps requires finitely many Sym-orbits of variables ([Draisma–Eggermont–Krone–Leykin](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.0828)); the notes' analysis is that the full claw family has infinitely many orbits (one per zero-sum multiset), so the theorem covers only the bounded-support subfamily and is non-effective even there. The independent set theorem lets state counts grow with the number of factors fixed, the opposite regime ([Hillar–Sullivant](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.1777)). Whether every invariant chain of toric ideals stabilises is itself posed as open ([Hillar–Martín del Campo](https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.0785)). On the tooling side there is real progress: fibre-graph and indispensable-binomial computation in Macaulay2 ([Clarke–Milner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19031)), multigraded implicitization that is parallel over multidegrees ([Cummings–Hollering](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsc.2025.102459)), group-based model types in OSCAR ([Boege et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15807)), and a Lean 4 formalisation of the Diaconis–Sturmfels theorem that a move set is a Markov basis if and only if it generates the toric ideal ([atlas-lean](https://github.com/facebookresearch/atlas-lean/blob/main/MathlibExt/RingTheory/MarkovBasis.lean)).

## Five attack routes, ranked by yield per unit effort

The routes below are the notes' proposals; their ordering and risk assessments are judgements, not results.

| Route | First concrete step | Targets | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Fixed-n computation | Markov basis of the 20×256 configuration (Z/4, five leaves); then six leaves by regularity plus faces | (a); first open instance of (b) | Cost; no proved degree cap for n ≥ 5 since normality of P_{Z/4,n} is open there |
| 2. Effective induction for Z/4 | Kimura-3 architecture with Z/4 exchange lemmas, local cases discharged by computer search | (a) for all n | Case explosion; threshold n_0 above computable base cases |
| 3. Saturation | Test whether the degree-≤4 ideal J_n satisfies J_n : q_0 = J_n at n = 3, 4 | (a) | Non-zero-divisor statements are as hard as primality of J_n |
| 4. Column alignment / intersection conjecture | Test "two columns can be aligned by low-degree moves" for small n; compute φ(Z/5, 5), φ(Z/6, 4), φ(Z/7, 4) | (b) | The alignment lemma is plausibly false for small n |
| 5. Refutation search | Indispensable-binomial and disconnected-fibre search for Z/4 at n = 5, 6 and for jumps φ(G, 4) > φ(G, 3) | both | Negative results are uninformative unless exhaustive |

**Route 1 is the obvious first move.** The Kimura-3 problem of identical size succeeded, and the aborted session run suggests Z/4 is cheaper; a degree-5 generator at five leaves would refute (a) and (b) at once, and a clean result supplies the base case every proof strategy needs. Six leaves (1024 variables) is infeasible by brute force on the Kimura-3 authors' experience; their workaround needs normality of the six-leaf polytope, which for Z/4 is known only at three and four leaves ([Michałek–Ventura 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584); [Dinu–Vodička](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05301)). A fibre-by-fibre, symmetry-reduced computation avoids the global Gröbner computation but terminates as a proof only with a degree cap.

**Route 2 is the only route with a track record**, and the only one that settles (a) for all n. Its realistic form is a redesign in which humans supply the potential function and the merge step while a computer discharges the local case analysis on a few special rows and columns: "there is a move of degree ≤ 4 that decreases the potential, or else a counting contradiction". The new ingredients are a Z/4 exchange lemma (three disagreements, one odd), a treatment of rows congruent mod 2, replacements for the Difference and Standard Lemmas, and the six-leaf base case. The quotient Z/4 → Z/2 with kernel {0, 2} suggests a two-level argument combining the quadratic Z/2 theorem with Kimura-3-style arguments on the subgroup; no source proposes this and it is speculation.

**Route 3 converts existing theory if it works.** Michałek's Lemma 7.2 already reduces any relation, after multiplying by a power of the neutral variable, to flows of support ≤ D(Z/4) = 4 using quadrics; a proof that the degree-≤4 ideal is saturated with respect to that variable would upgrade a scheme-theoretic statement to (a). The viability test at n = 3, 4 is a small Macaulay2 computation that has not been run. A cheaper publishable intermediate is the scheme-theoretic degree-4 statement for Z/4 in Donten-Bury's style, with six types of minimal zero-sum flows, (1,3), (2,2), (1,1,2), (3,3,2), (1,1,1,1), (3,3,3,3), instead of three; no paper does this for Z/4.

**Route 4 attacks constancy directly** and is where the cleanest general theorem would come from: if for every n ≥ 3 low-degree moves can make two columns agree row-by-row in both tables, the Michałek–Ventura argument gives φ(G, n+1) ≤ φ(G, n) verbatim. The intersection conjecture at n = 4, 5 for Z/4 (is the claw ideal the sum of the ideals of its two-inner-vertex prolongations?) is a finite check and would be informative evidence. **Route 5 is cheap insurance** using exactly the certificate format of the Z/17, Z/13 and Z/12 examples, with the caveat that disconnected fibres need not be two-element.

Across all routes, results should ship with certificates: lower bounds as explicit fibres, which are now demonstrably Lean-checkable, and fixed-n upper bounds as fibre-connectivity data checkable against a formal statement of the Markov-basis theorem.

## Conclusion

The question has quietly changed shape. Before September 2026, "degree ≤ 4 for Z/4" read as one small instance of a believed law φ(G) ≤ |G|; that law is false at orders 12, 13, 17 and 19, the first violating order lies between 9 and 12, and equations of degree ≤ |G| can fail even set-theoretically on the boundary. What survives is a sharper and more local problem: for Z/4, the degree bound is constancy, constancy is the single inequality φ(Z/4, n+1) ≤ φ(Z/4, n), and Z/4 is the smallest group where that inequality is unknown. The evidence in favour is specific to small groups (exact values at three and four leaves, normality at three and four leaves, the proved Z/2×Z/2 twin, an exhaustively starved counterexample mechanism), which means any proof must also be specific to small groups, as both existing proofs are.

The honest state of the art is that a single overnight-to-weeks computation, φ(Z/4, 5), is the most informative missing fact, and it has not been published in twenty-two years. The counterexamples also supply the working method for what follows: explicit finite certificates, independently enumerated and formally checked, in a subject whose two hand proofs their own author considers nearly unreadable. A Z/4 theorem several times the size of the Kimura-3 proof will only be credible in that form, and the same infrastructure would decide at once whether constancy survives its first real tests at φ(Z/5, 5), φ(Z/6, 4) and φ(Z/7, 4).
