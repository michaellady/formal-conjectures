## 1. The combinatorial language: flows, tables, compatible multisets, exchanges, moves of degree d — dictionary with ideal generation

### Takeaway
The whole problem is a statement about d×n tables of group elements whose rows sum to 0: the ideal of X(K_{1,n},G) is generated in degree ≤ d iff any two tables with the same column multisets can be connected by steps that each replace at most d rows by a compatible set of rows. This dictionary is group-independent and is stated (not really "proved") in [M-Z3] Section 2 and [MV-K3P] Section 2; it rests on the toric structure from the discrete Fourier transform and on Sturmfels' Lemma 4.1 for toric ideals.

### Cited Findings
- Flow: "A group-based flow f (on n) or simply a flow is a function f : [n] → G represented by an n-tuple of group elements ... and satisfying Σ f(i) = 0 ∈ G. With a coordinate-wise action flows form a group of flows G isomorphic to G^{n−1}." (Def. 2.1) — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)
- Polytope P_{n,G}: lattice M ≅ Z^{|G|}, M^n with basis e_(i,g); flow f ↦ Σ_i e_(i,f(i)); the images are the vertices (Def. 2.2). "They are subpolytopes of a unit cube, hence all their integral points are vertices." The toric variety of P_{n,G} "is isomorphic to X(K_{1,n},G)". — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)
- Normality is NOT automatic: "The polytope P_{n,G} does not have to be normal (however for many groups it is conjectured to be)." (Section 2) — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)
- Compatibility: "two multisets of flows (on n) M1 and M2 are compatible if for any i ∈ [n] we have ∪_{f∈M1}{f(i)} = ∪_{g∈M2}{g(i)} as multisets of elements of G. This is equivalent to the fact that corresponding vertices sum up to the same lattice element." Degree of the binomial = cardinality of either multiset. (Section 2) — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)
- The dictionary, verbatim: "The ideal of the variety X(K_{1,n},G) is generated in degree d if and only if the compatiblilty relation on multisets equals the transitive closure of the restriction of compatibility relation to multisets of cardinality at most d and the operation of adding a flow. More explicitly, if and only if we are able to pass from any multiset to any compatible multiset in a series of steps, each time exchanging a submultiset (of one multiset) with at most d flows by a compatible multiset of flows." (Section 2; stated without separate proof) — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)
- Quadratic exchange on an index set: "By exchanging two flows f, g (in one multiset) on a set of indices I, we will mean replacing them with two flows f′, g′ such that f′(i) = f(i) and g′(i) = g(i) for i ∉ I, f′(i) = g(i) and g′(i) = f(i) for i ∈ I. Notice that this is only possible if Σ_{i∈I} f(i) = Σ_{i∈I} g(i)." (Section 2) — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)
- Table version: binomials = pairs of tables T0, T1 up to row permutation; rows = flows = variables; columns = leaves. "a binomial belongs to I(X(G,K_{1,n})) if and only if the two tables representing it are compatible, i.e., for each i, the ith column of T0 and the ith column of T1 are equal as multisets." "The procedure consisting of selecting a subset of rows in T of cardinality at most d, and replacing it with a compatible set of rows is a move of degree d. A binomial, represented by a pair of tables T0, T1 ..., is generated by binomials of degree at most d if and only if there exists a finite sequence of moves of degree d applied to T0 or T1 that transform T0 into T1." (Section 2, "Binomials, tables, and moves") — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)
- Symmetries acting on the tables/ideal: "the group S_n, the group of flows G, and the automorphism group Aut(G) all act on the ideals of these varieties" (Remark 2.2, Remark 2.5): S_n permutes columns; a flow is added to every row of both tables; Aut(G) is applied entrywise. — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)
- Hamming distance / disagreement string (Def. 2.6): for rows r0 = (g_i + a_i), r1 = (g_i), the multiset {a_ℓ : a_ℓ ≠ 0} is the disagreement string, its size the Hamming distance; "Up to the action of the group of flows G on both flows, we may assume that the group elements g_i = 0 for all i." — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)
- Counting functions (Section 2): for x ∈ G, x_{i1...ik} = number of copies of x in columns i1..ik; any integer combination takes the same total value on T0 and T1 because they are compatible; algebraically "a counting function defines a grading of the variables, that is a specialization of the multi-grading". — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)
- Where the underlying algebra is proved: toric structure via Discrete Fourier Transform (attributed to Hendy–Penny, Erdős–Székely–Steel, and in generality to [33, 41] = Michałek 2011, Sturmfels–Sullivant); reduction from arbitrary trees to claw trees K_{1,n} by [SS, Theorem 26] / toric fiber products [Sul07, Cor. 2.11]. — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584), [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)
- Donten-Bury's version cites the source of the binomial/relation dictionary: "by [13, Lem. 4.1] we can express generators of I as relations ΣL_i = ΣR_j, where L = {L_i} and R = {R_j} are finite multisets of group-based flows of the same cardinality" ([13] = Sturmfels, Gröbner bases and convex polytopes). She also notes that P "has to be considered in a sublattice Λ_P spanned by its vertices to obtain the correct toric structure." (Section 2.2) — [DB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4169)
- Tree reduction is group-independent also scheme-theoretically: "in the proof of Proposition 2.1 we do not use any information on the group G, hence for any finite abelian group G it is sufficient to prove the scheme-theoretic version of [14, Conj. 29] just for claw trees." — [DB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4169)
- The universal "column-merging" lemma (reduction n → n−1), K3P form (Theorem 3.4): "Suppose that a pair of compatible tables T0, T1 with n ≥ 7 columns do not contain rows with bad pairs. Then the corresponding binomial is generated in degree at most φ(G, K_{1,n−1})." Proof: sum columns n−1 and n; the new tables are compatible with n−1 columns; lift each move; then "we make quadratic moves to adjust the elements in columns n − 1 and n". — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)
- General-G form of the same step (proof of Theorem 3.12): if two columns c_j, c_{j+1} agree row-by-row between T0 and T1, sum them, apply φ(G,n−1), lift, and fix the two columns with quadratic moves (if a_{jl} ≠ a_{kl} then a_{jl}+b_{jl} = a_{kl}+b_{kl} and a quadratic move repairs it). — [MV-fin](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07263)

### Inferences
- Everything in this section is valid verbatim for G = Z/4: flows, tables, compatibility, moves, counting functions, the three symmetry groups, and the column-merging lemma. The only group-specific input to column merging is the hypothesis that makes the merged tables compatible (see Section 5 below).
- A precise formulation of the hypothesis needed for merging columns i, j (extracted from [M-Z3] Lemma 4.15 and [MV-K3P] Thm 3.4): the multisets of pairs {(r(i), r(j))} must coincide in T0 and T1. Sufficient conditions used: (a) K3P: no row has both entries nonzero ("no bad pairs"); (b) Z3: every row agrees with a fixed template h in at least one of the two columns; (c) general G: the two columns agree row-by-row.

### Gaps
- Neither [M-Z3] nor [MV-K3P] writes out a proof of the "generated in degree d ⇔ connected by degree-d moves" dictionary; it is treated as standard (Sturmfels' book, Lemma 4.1 / Markov basis theory). I did not open Sturmfels' book to quote it.

## 2. The Z/3 proof ([M-Z3] Section 4): structure, inductions, lemmas, where primality/smallness and cubic moves are used

### Takeaway
Triple induction: on n (base n < 7 by computer, from Donten-Bury–Michałek), on degree d > 3, and on the number k−1 of agreements between a chosen flow f1 ∈ M1 and g1 = 0 ∈ M2. Either a short move increases the agreement (then remove the common row and induct on d), or the structure is so rigid that all flows nearly coincide with a template off a few columns, in which case two columns are merged and one inducts on n. The author calls the proof "very technical" and says it cannot be followed "without a pen and a sheet of paper".

### Cited Findings

**Statement and base.**
- "Theorem 1.4. The phylogenetic complexity of the group Z3 equals 3." — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)
- "Our proof is inductive on n, the length of the flows. For fixed n we show inductively that any binomial of degree d > 3 is generated by cubics. For n < 7 it was known before that the ideal of Z3 is generated by cubics [DBM12]. Thus we assume n ≥ 7." Computational tools named for small n: "[BTRS01, tt, DBM12]" = Normaliz, 4ti2, Donten-Bury–Michałek. — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)
- Normalisations: "by the action of G we may assume that g1 = 0 ... Suppose that f1 and g1 agree on k − 1 indices. Our aim is to increase k, until k − 1 = n ... By the action of the symmetric group S_n we may assume that f1(i) = 0 if and only if 1 ≤ i ≤ k − 1." Proofs are organised as case trees, "depth-first, left-first". — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)
- Author's own description of method: "For large n the basic distinction into the case where two flows are very different or very much alike remains valid. However, in both cases (especially when two flows are much alike) we failed to find short, easy proofs or conclude simply by deeper and deeper case study. Our arguments rely on a mixture of counting arguments and combinatorial tricks." — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)

**The key prime-specific tool (Section 3, reused in Section 4).**
- "Lemma 3.1. Fix G = Z_p for p a prime number. Suppose there exist two flows f, g and a set of indices I of cardinality p − 1 such that for each i ∈ I we have f(i) ≠ g(i). Then for any subset of indices I′ disjoint from I there exists a subset I″ ⊆ I such that we can exchange f and g on I′ ∪ I″." Proof by Alon's Combinatorial Nullstellensatz over the field Z_p (an inductive proof is "left as an exercise"). For p = 3 this says: two flows differing in 2 places can be exchanged on any prescribed further set of columns, at the cost of also swapping some of those 2 places. — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)

**Top-level case split.**
- "4.1. Suppose k ≠ n − 1. ... Either f1 attains the same element for at least three indices greater or equal to k or k = n − 3 and we can assume f1(k) = f1(k+1) = 1, f1(k+2) = f1(k+3) = 2." "4.2. Suppose k = n − 1 ... We can also assume f1 = (0,...,0,1,2)." — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)

**Subsection 4.1.1: f1 = 111 on I2 = {k,k+1,k+2}** ("by the action of the nontrivial automorphism of Z3"); I1 = {1..k−1}, I3 = {k+3..n}.
- Lemma 4.1: "If there exists a flow f ∈ M1 that has either (i) both 0 and 2 or (ii) only 0, in the image of I2, then we can increase k." (quadratic exchange with f1)
- Remark 4.2: for each i ∈ I2 some f ∈ M1 has f(i) = 0, and such f restricted to I2 is {1,1,0} or {0,0,1}.
- Lemma 4.3: "We may assume that: (i) there is no flow among M1 that associates three times 2 on I2, (ii) there exist at least as many flows in M1 that associate to I2 exactly one 1 as those that associate three times 1." (ii) is an averaging argument across M1 vs M2.
- Proposition 4.4 (the "crucial proposition"): "We can compatibly change M1, so that there exists a function h : I1 ∪ I3 → Z3 such that: (i) for every index j0 ∈ I3 we have h(j0) = f1(j0), (ii) for any f ∈ M1 \ f1 there exists at most one index j0 ∈ I1 ∪ I3 such that h(j0) ≠ f(j0), as otherwise we are able to increase k."
- Case 1 (some f2 with f2|I2 = 001; h := f2|_{I1∪I3}): Lemma 4.5 ("For any i ∈ I3 we can assume f1(i) = f2(i). For any i ∈ I1 we have either f2(i) = 0 or f2(i) = 1. The number of i ∈ I1 such that f2(i) = 1 equals 2 modulo 3."); Corollary 4.6; Lemma 4.7 ("no flows in M1 that to I2 associate twice 2 and once 1").
- Case 1a (f3|I2 = 110): Lemma 4.8 ("any two flows f_{i1} and f_{i2} such that Σ_{I2} f_{i1} = 1 ... and Σ_{I2} f_{i2} = 2 ... differ on I1 ∪ I3 on precisely one index j for which f_{i1}(j) = f_{i2}(j) − 2"); Definition 4.9 (distinguished index j); Corollary 4.10 ("All flows f ∈ M1 such that Σ_{i=k}^{k+2} f(i) = 1 agree on I1 ∪ I3"); flows with I2-sum 0 are paired with a flow having a single 1 on I2 (via Lemma 4.3) and adjusted pairwise.
- Case 1b (no flow with 110 pattern): Lemma 4.11 ("all f ∈ M1 such that Σ_{I2} f(i) = 1 agree on I1 ∪ I3"); Lemma 4.12 (I2-sum 0 ⇒ agrees with h apart from one index); Lemma 4.13 (I2-sum 2 ⇒ agrees with h apart from one index). Lemma 4.13 contains the only cubic move of subsection 4.1.1: "We can apply the cubic relation among f1, f2, f that on indices i, k, k+1 is given by (0,1,1),(1,0,0),(2,0,1) = (2,0,0),(0,0,1),(1,1,1)", plus an internal Claim proved by averaging the number of 1's on four columns.
- Case 2 (no flow is 001-type on I2): Lemma 4.14 (image of I2 is one of {1,1,1},{1,1,0},{1,1,2},{1,2,2}); contradiction by pairing flows of M1 and M2 and counting 0's on I2.
- Closing 4.1.1: Prop. 4.4 gives templates h for M1 and h′ for M2; "on I1 ∪ I3 the functions h and h′ can disagree only on at most 2 indices ... Hence, for n ≥ 7 we can find two indices i, j ∈ I1 ∪ I3 on which h and h′ agree." Lemma 4.15: "For i, j as above the two multisets of pairs {(f(i), f(j)) : f ∈ M1}, {(g(i), g(j)) : g ∈ M2} are equal." Then: "we can sum up entries on indices i, j obtaining two compatible multisets M1′, M2′ of flows of size n − 1. By induction, such relation can be generated and we can lift each quadric and cubic in the generation process. After this procedure, it is enough to apply quadric relations, exchanging only flows on indices i, j".
— all from [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)

**Subsection 4.1.2: k = n−3, f1 = (0,…,0,1,1,2,2).**
- Lemma 4.16 (a list of forbidden two-column patterns in M1 and M2, each killed by one quadratic exchange); Case 1 (some f2 with 00 on k,k+1): Lemma 4.17 ("We may assume f2(k+2) = f2(k+3) = 2"), Lemma 4.18; then a counting contradiction; Case 2: direct counting contradiction. Only quadratic moves. — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)

**Subsection 4.2: k = n−1, f1 = (0,…,0,1,2)** (Hamming distance 2; uses Definition 4.19 "type (a,b)" = values in the last two columns).
- Lemma 4.20: "If there exists a flow (i) g ∈ M2 of type (1,2) or (ii) f ∈ M1 of type (0,0), then we can increase k."
- Proposition 4.21 (goal): "We can assume that for each flow f ∈ M1 different from f1 there exists at most one index i < n − 1 such that f(i) ≠ f2(i)."
- Case 1 (some flow of type (2,1), wlog g4 ∈ M2): Lemma 4.22 (no type (2,1) in M1). Case 1a (g2 of type (1,0)): Lemma 4.23 (no type (0,2) in M2; cubic move (2,1),(0,2),(1,0) = (1,2),(2,0),(0,1)); Lemma 4.24 (no type (0,1) in M1; cubic move); Remark 4.25; then Steps 1–4 by type, each using Lemma 3.1. Case 1b (no (1,0) in M2): Corollary 4.26, Lemma 4.27, Lemma 4.28 (cubic), then a type-by-type argument ending with a cubic move among f′, f3, f1 on I ∪ {n−1,n}.
- Case 2 (no type (2,1) anywhere): Case 2a (g2 of type (1,1)): Lemma 4.29 (cubic (0,0),(1,1),(2,2) = (1,2),(2,0),(0,1)), Lemma 4.30 (cubic), Lemma 4.31 (f2,f3 agree off the last two columns; same for g2,g3 — via Lemma 3.1), Lemma 4.32, Lemma 4.33, then a linear counting contradiction with unknowns x,y,z,w,q. Case 2b reduces to 2a "by the nontrivial automorphism of Z3" or "by the transposition of n−1 and n", else a counting contradiction.
- Closing 4.2: choose, among multisets reachable from M2 \ g1 by cubic moves, one minimising the number s of indices < n−1 where a flow can differ from f2; show s ≤ 3; treat s = 3, 2, 1, 0; in each case find i, j and "proceed exactly as in Lemma 4.15 and the paragraph after it" (merge two columns, induct on n).
— all from [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)

**Where "3 is prime" / "the group is tiny" is used (as stated in the paper).**
- Lemma 3.1 is stated only for Z_p and proved with the Combinatorial Nullstellensatz over the field Z_p; it is invoked throughout subsection 4.2 (Steps 1–4 of Case 1a, Case 1b, Lemmas 4.31, 4.32, and the s = 2 closing step). — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)
- Automorphism transitivity: Section 3 Case 1 uses "As all nonzero elements of G are indistinguishable"; Section 4 uses "the nontrivial automorphism of Z3" to normalise f1|I2 = 111 (4.1.1) and in Case 2b of 4.2. — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)
- Smallness: the initial dichotomy in 4.1 (three equal nonzero entries, or exactly 1,1,2,2) is a pigeonhole on two nonzero elements; all sub-cases enumerate patterns over {0,1,2} on 2–3 columns (e.g. Lemma 4.14 lists four multisets; subsection 4.2 has nine "types"). — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)

### Inferences
- The proof never reduces Hamming distance step by step (as the K3P proof does); instead each top-level case (distance ≥ 3 with 111; distance 4 with 1122; distance 2) either increases agreement or directly reaches the merge-two-columns situation. The "template h + at most one deviation" statements (Prop. 4.4, Prop. 4.21) are the Z3 analogue of the K3P "no bad pairs" condition.
- Cubic (degree-3) moves are needed only at isolated points: Lemma 4.13 and the six-or-so places in subsection 4.2 listed above (all on the last two columns, i.e. in the Hamming-distance-2 case). Everything else is quadratic exchanges plus double counting. By analogy, in a Z/4 attempt degree-4 moves should be expected mainly in the distance-2 cases.
- For p = 3, Lemma 3.1 reduces to the fact that two nonzero differences d1, d2 ∈ Z3 have subset sums covering all of Z3 (if d1 = d2: {0, d, 2d}; if d1 = −d2: {0, d1, d2}). This is exactly the property that Z/4 lacks (see Section 5).

### Gaps
- The Z3 base case n < 7 is delegated to [DBM12] (Donten-Bury–Michałek, J. Algebr. Stat. 2012); I did not read that paper, so I cannot say how (computationally) n = 6 was done for Z3 (3^5 = 243 variables).
- The paper leaves several lemma proofs as "similar and easy" (e.g. Lemma 4.16); I did not re-derive them.

## 3. The Z/2×Z/2 proof ([MV-K3P]): architecture, size, cases, computer assistance, base cases, reduction of n, use of group structure, remarks on generalising

### Takeaway
A 32-page paper devoted "solely to the proof of the main theorem", structured as nested inductions ("Matryoshka"): on n (base n ≤ 5 by 4ti2 on a 1 TB RAM server; n = 6 by a hybrid of Normaliz regularity bounds, hand arguments and 4ti2 on faces; n ≥ 7 by hand), on degree d ≥ 5, and on the Hamming distance k between two chosen rows. k ≥ 3 is reduced to k = 2 (five disagreement strings); k = 2 (string αα) splits into ten cases I–X, of which seven are reduced to a three-case "main case" where "bad pairs" in two agreement columns are eliminated and the columns merged. The proof leans heavily on Aut(G) ≅ S3, on every element being an involution, and on the three quotient maps to Z2. The authors say nothing explicit about Z/4 or other groups in this paper.

### Cited Findings

**Statement, length, scope.**
- "Main Theorem. The phylogenetic complexity of the Kimura 3-parameter model φ(Z2 × Z2) equals four." "The whole article is devoted solely to the proof of the main theorem." The arXiv version is 32 pages; the proof occupies Sections 3.1–3.3 (pp. 6–28) plus Appendix 4. — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)
- Notation: elements 0, α, β, γ; "Aut(G) ≅ S3"; group homomorphisms "φ_g : Z2 × Z2 → Z2, the group homomorphism given by the quotient map ... modulo the subgroup generated by the element g". — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)

**Architecture (Section 3.1).**
- "Our proof is an induction on the number of leaves n ... The base of our induction is n = 3. The case of n ≤ 5 leaves has been studied computationally. More precisely, for n = 3 the result is presented in [41] and for n = 4 it is computed in [16]. For n = 5 we used the program featured in [16] to produce the vertices of the polytope. The computer algebra program 4ti2 [47] ... was able to compute the Markov basis using a server equipped with a CPU 4 Intel-Xeon E7-8837/32 cores/2.67GHz and a memory of 1024Gb RAM." — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)
- "Proposition 3.2. The ideal I(X(G, K_{1,5})) is minimally generated by 22240 polynomials of degree at most four: 12960 quadrics, 2560 cubics, and 6720 quartics." — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)
- n = 6 could not be done by brute force: "we were not able to address the case n = 6 only with computational tools. Based on our experiments with 4ti2, we expect the computation to be not feasible: it would run for several years on a server of the same capability as the one mentioned above, and a memory of 1Tb RAM would not be sufficient". — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)
- "For n ≥ 7, we have an induction on the degree d of the generators ... Inside a specific degree d, we have an induction on the Hamming distance k of two rows of the tables. ... Suppose we have a binomial generator of degree d ≥ 5. ... Two rows have Hamming distance k and we reduce it to k = 0; ... the given pair of tables is transformed into a pair of tables that have an identical row. ... By induction on d, such a binomial can be generated in degree at most 4." "Recall that there do not exist flows whose Hamming distance is k = 1". — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)
- Choice of rows (Remark 2.7): "we may choose as first rows of T0 and T1 two rows that minimize the Hamming distance among all the pairs of rows from T0 and T1." — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)
- k = 2 is "the technical heart of the proof": "We identify ten cases, indexed by roman numerals ... Here we provide a uniform proof for three crucial cases: Case I, II, and III. ... we refer to those as the 'main case'. The rest of the cases is treated by reducing them to the main case." — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)
- How n is reduced: in the main case look at the second rows r_{0x}, r_{αz}; by Corollary 3.6 their disagreement outside columns 1,2 has length ℓ ≤ 3, so "since n ≥ 7, the length of the agreement string ... outside columns 1 and 2, is at least n − 5 ≥ 2"; take columns n−1, n in the agreement string and eliminate "bad pairs". "Definition 3.3 (Bad pairs). A bad pair is a string xy, where the elements x, y ∈ G are such that: (i) they are both nonzero; (ii) x is in column n − 1 and y is in column n." Then Theorem 3.4 (quoted in Section 1 above) merges the two columns. — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)

**Section 3.2: Hamming distance ≥ 3 → 2.**
- "Proposition 3.5. The disagreement strings (i) αααα, (ii) ααββ, (iii) ααβγ, and (iv) αααβ can be reduced." Each case: choose a counting function (e.g. 0_{1234} − α_{1234}), normalise its sign with the flow action, find a row where it has the "wrong" sign, and exclude all its possible contents by quadratic moves (two cubic moves appear, in (i) and (iv)); (ii) is reduced to (i) by acting with the flow (0,0,γ,γ). — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)
- "Corollary 3.6. Suppose that a table T contains two rows r and r′ having disagreement string of cardinality four. Then, using moves if degree at most three, T can be transformed in such a way that the disagreement string has cardinality at most three. Moreover, only the four columns of the disagreement string are involved in the reduction." ### Inferences
- The four strings in Prop. 3.5 / Cor. 3.6 are not all zero-sum (ααβγ sums to α, αααβ sums to γ), so they cannot be complete disagreement strings of two flows; they must be read as "any four columns of a disagreement string of length ≥ 4". This is consistent with the sentence introducing Prop. 3.5 ("the disagreement string is given by at least four entries") and with the four strings being exactly the orbits of 4-multisets of nonzero elements under Aut(G) ≅ S3. So the K3P reduction works locally on any 4 columns of disagreement — a design feature worth copying.
- Case count for the K3P proof, by my tally from the text: 4 (length-4 strings) + 1 (αβγ, six lemmas) + 10 (Cases I–X for αα; 7 reduced to 3) + for the main case 5 "no bad pair" lemmas, the hardest (Lemma 3.32) with 4 steps × 3 cases × 2 variants ("resp."), + a separate 4-step treatment of n = 6. Computer assistance: 4ti2 Markov bases for n = 5 (1 TB RAM) and for two 6-leaf faces; Normaliz Hilbert series/normality for three 6-leaf polytopes. There is no computer-checked case analysis of the hand proof.
- Lemma 3.24 contains a move printed as "000 + αβγ + β0β = α0α + ββ0 + 0ββ + 0γ" (three rows on the left, four on the right); this appears to be a misprint for a quartic move with a fourth row "+ βα" or similar (compare the next line). Anyone formalising the argument will have to repair such details.

## 4. The scheme-theoretic Z/3 result ([DB]): method and why it is weaker/different

### Takeaway
Donten-Bury proves only that the degree ≤ 3 binomials cut out the right projective scheme (I′ : J^∞ = I), not that they generate I. Saturation lets her multiply by powers of the variable x_0 of the trivial flow, i.e. freely add copies of the zero row to both tables; with zero rows available every flow splits into "pairs" and "triples" (minimal zero-sum pieces), and the proof becomes a finite case analysis on configurations of pairs and triples with induction on total support size.

### Cited Findings
- "Theorem 1.1. For G ≃ Z3 and any tree T the projective schemes defined by the ideal I of phylogenetic invariants of the corresponding model and its subideal I′ generated by elements of degree 2 and 3 are the same. That is, the saturation of I′ with respect to the irrelevant ideal is equal to I." "Note that this result implies also the set-theoretic one". — [DB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4169)
- Why saturation helps (Section 2.3): a ∈ I′ : J^∞ iff for each variable some power times a lies in I′; "by [11, Lem. 4.3] the situation is symmetric with respect to permutations of variables, hence it is sufficient to prove this statement for one chosen variable. We may choose the one corresponding to the trivial flow ... multiplying an element of I ... by a power of this variable is adding the same number of copies of the trivial flow to L and R." — [DB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4169)
- "Lemma 2.4. Let a be an element of the ideal I of phylogenetic invariants for Z3, represented by multisets of flows L and R. Then there is n ∈ N such that x_0^n a can be decomposed as a sum of an element of I′ and an element of I represented by L′ and R′ which consist only of flows of two types: (1) pairs – flows with only two nontrivial entries, (2) triples – flows with only three nontrivial entries." Proof: any Z3-flow has a consecutive block with 2 or 3 nonzero entries summing to 0; split it off with the quadric L1 + 0 = L1′ + L1″. — [DB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4169)
- Generality remark: "The idea of decomposing flows into simpler ones after adding the trivial flow works analogously for any finite abelian group, see [10, Lem. 7.2], but in the case of Z3 (and other groups of small order) the set of possible basic configurations is relatively simple and allows us to proceed with a combinatorial argument." — [DB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4169)
- Notation 2.6: one type of pair (a,b) (g1 at a, g2 at b; ordered) and two types of triples (a,b,c)_{g1}, (a,b,c)_{g2}. "Lemma 2.7. If R or L contains both a g1-triple (a,b,c)_{g1} and a g2-triple (x,y,z)_{g2}, we may replace them with pairs (possibly after adding a trivial flow to both sides of the relation)" via the cubic "(a,b,c)_{g1} + (x,y,z)_{g2} + 0 = (a,x) + (b,y) + (c,z)". — [DB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4169)
- Induction (Section 2.5): "not on the standard degree, because we need to multiply the relation by the variable corresponding to the trivial flow ... We use the grading by the size of the flow: a variable is in the d-th graded piece if the corresponding flow has d nontrivial entries." Basic step = "use a relation" of degree ≤ 3 on one side to create a common flow, then "delete" it (Notation 2.5, 2.10). — [DB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4169)
- Case structure: Section 3 no pairs (3.1, 3.2); Section 4 two pairs with four different indices (4.1.1, 4.1.2, 4.2, 4.3); Section 5 two pairs with three different indices (5.1.1–5.1.3, 5.2.1–5.2.3); Section 6 only two different indices in pairs. The recurring tool is the counting Lemma 3.1: "Assume that L and R consist only of pairs and triples, and • if α appears in L in a flow with g_m, then this flow contains also β with g_k, • if β appears in R in a flow with g_k, then this flow contains also α with g_m. Then β cannot appear in L with g_k in a flow which does not contain α with g_m." — [DB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4169)
- Cubic relations among pairs are needed, e.g. "(1,2) + (a,1) + (2,a) = (1,a) + (2,1) + (a,2)" (5.2.1) and "(x,2) + (s,b) + (2,1) = (x,1) + (2,b) + (s,2)" (4.1.2). — [DB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4169)
- Link to matroids in the "no pairs" case: "the fact that in this case the relations can be generated in degree 2 is a consequence of a result on uniform (or, much more generally, strongly base orderable) matroids. This is a special case of the White's conjecture". — [DB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4169)
- Lineage: "a combinatorial proof of the scheme-theoretic version for G ≃ Z3, using ideas similar to those presented in [11]" where [11] = Michałek, "Constructive degree bounds for group-based models" (JCTA 2013), which "proves the scheme-theoretical version for the 3-Kimura model, and also that for a fixed abelian group G there is a bound on the degree in which I is generated, independent on the size of the tree" [as phrased by Donten-Bury; Michałek's own table says the general-G result of [Mic13] is at the level of the projective scheme]. — [DB](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.4169)
- Position in the hierarchy (Michałek's table): rows from finest to coarsest are Gröbner basis / generators of the ideal / the projective scheme / set-theoretically / on a Zariski open subset; "the higher the row, the finer algebraic properties are required." For general G: projective scheme — "finite by [Mic13]"; Zariski open subset — "degree ≤ |G| [CFSM14]". — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)

### Inferences
- Why it is weaker: saturation with respect to the irrelevant ideal only gives f·x^N ∈ I′ for large N. In table language, one is allowed to append arbitrarily many zero rows to both tables before connecting them by degree-3 moves. The ideal-theoretic statement forbids this: the tables must be connected at their original size. The entire difficulty of [M-Z3] Section 4 (no zero rows available; rows cannot be split) is absent in [DB]; conversely [DB]'s argument needs no induction on n and no computer base case.
- For Z/4 the analogous building blocks would be the minimal zero-sum sequences of Z/4: (1,3), (2,2), (1,1,2), (3,3,2), (1,1,1,1), (3,3,3,3) — six types (two kinds of pairs, two of triples, two of quadruples) instead of three. Lemma 2.7's analogue (eliminating "opposite" pieces on one side) and the case analysis by pair configurations would need to be redone with (2,2)-pairs, which are unordered and interact with both orientations. This looks feasible but considerably longer; it would give only the scheme-theoretic statement for Z/4.
- By Michałek's table, for Z/4 the statement "degree ≤ |G| on a Zariski open subset" is already covered by [CFSM14] (cited, not read here), and finiteness scheme-theoretically by [Mic13]. A scheme-theoretic degree-4 result for Z/4 is not mentioned in any of the papers read.

### Gaps
- I did not read [Mic13] (the K3P scheme-theoretic proof and the general Lemma 7.2), so I cannot state exactly how its method differs from [DB] or whether it already implies a concrete scheme-theoretic bound for Z/4.
- Whether anyone has proved the scheme-theoretic degree-4 statement for Z/4 was not searched for (outside my assigned sources).

## 5. Which parts are group-independent (reusable for Z/4) and which rely on properties Z/4 lacks

### Takeaway
The framework (tables, moves, symmetry normalisations, counting functions, triple induction on n / d / Hamming distance, column merging, computational base cases) is group-independent. What is group-specific is every "local exchange" lemma and every enumeration: Z/3 uses that p−1 = 2 disagreements allow arbitrary exchanges (primality); K3P uses x+x = 0, α+β+γ = 0 and a transitive automorphism group. Z/4 has neither: it has a characteristic subgroup {0,2}, Aut of order 2, and elements of different orders.

### Cited Findings
- Group-independent (stated for arbitrary finite abelian G): definitions of flows, compatibility, moves and the degree-d dictionary ([M-Z3] Section 2: "Throughout the article G will be a finite abelian group"); the three symmetry actions ([MV-K3P] Remark 2.2 is phrased for the family X(G,K_{1,n})). — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177), [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)
- Group-independent replacement for Lemma 3.1: "Lemma 3.3. Let f, f′ be flows. Let I be a subset of indices and suppose |I| ≥ |G|. There exists a (non-empty) subset I′ ⊂ I such that a quadratic move of f and f′ on I′ can be performed." (pigeonhole on partial sums). — [MV-fin](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07263)
- Group-independent n-stabilisation: "Theorem 3.12. For any finite abelian group G, the phylogenetic complexity φ(G) of G is finite." Proof shape: "Fix N ≫ |G| ... φ(G,N) is finite by the Hilbert Basis Theorem. Assume n > N. We will show that φ(G,n) ≤ φ(G,n − 1)." via Lemma 3.11 (two columns can be made to agree row-by-row using quadratic moves when n is sufficiently large) and column merging. The proof uses "frequent elements" with threshold F(G) > |G|² + 3|G| and Ramsey-type Lemmas 3.5, 3.9, 3.10; no explicit N is given in the passages I read. — [MV-fin](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07263)
- Prime-specific: Lemma 3.1 is stated for "G = Z_p for p a prime number" and proved via a polynomial in Z_p[x_i] (field needed for the Nullstellensatz). — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)
- Transitivity-specific: "As all nonzero elements of G are indistinguishable, we may assume that there are 3p indices i, such that f1(i) = 1." ([M-Z3] Section 3, Case 1); "Aut(G) ≅ S3" and "Up to the action of Aut(G), we may assume x = β" ([MV-K3P]). — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177), [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)
- Involution/sum-zero-specific: Standard Lemma 3.19's step "u ≠ v and they are both different from 0 and x. Hence, the sum u + v + x = 0". — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)
- Normality is used (and verified by computer for that one polytope) in the K3P n = 6 step (Prop. 3.25), while in general "The polytope P_{n,G} does not have to be normal". — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584), [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)
- Small-case data for Z/4 from the original paper (table in Section 5; the paper's tableaux there have n+1 columns, so its "n = 2, 3" appear to be 3 and 4 leaves): Z4, n=2: 0 quadrics, 16 cubics, 6 quartics, φ = 4; Z4, n=3: 344 quadrics, 256 cubics, 96 quartics, φ = 4. Compare Z2×Z2: n=2: 0/16/18, n=3: 360/261/480, φ = 4. Also Z5 n=2: φ = 4; Z6 n=2: φ = 6; Z7 n=2: φ = 6. — [SS](https://arxiv.org/abs/q-bio/0402015)

### Inferences (my own analysis; elementary hand computations)
Reusable verbatim for Z/4:
1. Tables/moves dictionary, symmetry normalisations (S_n, adding a flow, Aut), counting functions and double counting.
2. The triple induction skeleton: n (computer base, then merge two columns), degree d ≥ 5, Hamming distance k between a closest pair of rows; and "Hamming distance 1 is impossible".
3. Column merging (Theorem 3.4 of [MV-K3P] / Lemma 4.15 of [M-Z3]): the proof only needs equal multisets of pairs in the two columns; "no row has two nonzero entries in columns n−1, n" suffices for any G.
4. [MV-fin] Lemma 3.3 (≥ |G| = 4 disagreements ⇒ some nonempty exchangeable subset) and the eventual-constancy theorem.
5. Computational base cases have the same size as for K3P, since the number of variables is |G|^{n−1}: 64 (n=4), 256 (n=5), 1024 (n=6).

Not transferable / needs new ideas:
1. Lemma 3.1 fails for Z/4. Subset sums of differences d_i ∈ Z/4∖{0}: if all d_i = 2 the subset sums are only {0,2}, for any number of indices, so two flows that are congruent mod 2 can never be exchanged so as to realise an odd prescribed sum. Conversely, by direct enumeration, three nonzero differences not all equal to 2 already have subset sums covering Z/4 (e.g. {1,1,1}, {1,1,2}, {1,2,2}, {1,2,3}, {1,1,3}, {1,3,3} all give {0,1,2,3}), while two differences never suffice in general ({1,1} misses 3; {1,3} misses 2). So the correct Z/4 analogue is: "3 disagreements, at least one odd ⇒ arbitrary exchange"; pairs of rows agreeing mod 2 are a genuinely new obstruction class with no counterpart in Z/3. In K3P the corresponding obstruction (differences confined to a subgroup {0,g}) exists but is symmetric under Aut and is handled by the three maps φ_g.
2. Aut(Z/4) = {±1} has orbits {1,3} and {2} on nonzero elements, versus one orbit for Z/3 (Aut = Z/2 on 2 elements) and for K3P (S3 on 3 elements). Consequences by direct count: 4-letter disagreement sub-strings up to Aut: 9 classes for Z/4 (15 multisets over {1,2,3}; 3 fixed by negation) versus 4 for K3P (Prop. 3.5). Zero-sum strings of length 2: two inequivalent ones for Z/4, (1,3) and (2,2), versus one for Z/3 ((1,2)) and one for K3P (αα). So the "technical heart" (distance 2) doubles at the root, and each root has more sub-cases because the second/third rows range over 4 elements with only a Z/2 symmetry (K3P used Aut to fix x = β and got 10 cases).
3. Zero-sum strings of length 3 for Z/4: only (1,1,2) ≅ (3,3,2); of length 4: (1,1,1,1) ≅ (3,3,3,3), (1,1,3,3), (1,2,2,3), (2,2,2,2). Unlike K3P, a reduction "distance ≥ 3 → 2" must deal with strings such as 1111 where no two letters sum to zero and no three do either (minimal zero-sum of length 4 = Davenport constant of Z/4; for Z/3 and Z2×Z2 the Davenport constant is 3). In K3P every 4 letters contain a zero-sum pair or triple.
4. Standard Lemma 3.19 and the Difference Lemma 3.17 of [MV-K3P] rely on x = −x and α+β+γ = 0. In Z/4, 1+2+3 = 2 ≠ 0 and 1 ≠ −1, so "difference" becomes oriented and the three-distinct-elements trick disappears. These two lemmas are used on almost every page of Section 3.3 there, so that section cannot be ported; it must be redesigned.
5. Z/3-specific rigidity statements (Prop. 4.4, Prop. 4.21: "all flows agree with a template off the special columns except at one index") are derived from Lemma 3.1 with p = 3; for Z/4 the natural analogue would be "agree except at ≤ 2 indices, or agree mod 2", which weakens the final step (finding two columns to merge needs n larger and/or an extra argument for the mod-2 class).
6. Normality: for K3P at n = 6 it was simply computed. For Z/4 I found no statement in these sources about normality of P_{n,Z/4}; if the 6-leaf Z/4 polytope is not normal, the regularity shortcut of Prop. 3.25/Appendix (Hochster + a-invariant) is unavailable and the n = 6 base case needs another route (or the hand proof must be made to work from n = 6).

Partly transferable:
- The quotient Z/4 → Z/2 (kernel {0,2}) can play the role of a single φ_g for parity counting; and the image model Z/2 is the fully understood quadratic case ([SS] Theorem 26). There is no analogue of having three such quotients permuted by Aut.
- Elements of the subgroup {0,2} behave like K3P elements (2+2 = 0), so sub-arguments about (2,2) disagreement may resemble the αα case; arguments about (1,3) resemble the Z/3 case (1,2). This is a heuristic expectation, not something any source states.

### Gaps
- Normality of P_{n,Z/4} (for n = 3,…,6): not addressed in the three target papers. (Donten-Bury–Michałek 2012 and Buczyńska–Buczyński–Kubjas–Michałek 2013, cited in [M-Z3] as [DBM12], [BBKM13], are the likely places; not read here.)
- No explicit N for the stabilisation φ(G,n) = φ(G,N), n ≥ N, is extracted; from the passages read, [MV-fin]'s bound comes from Ramsey-type lemmas and is presumably astronomically large, so it does not reduce Z/4 to a finite computation in practice. I did not verify this by reading Lemmas 3.9–3.10 in full.
- Whether φ(Z/4, n) = 4 has been computed for n = 5 (256 variables, same size as the K3P n = 5 run) is not stated in the sources read.

## 6. Explicit statements by the authors about Z/4, Z/5, cyclic groups, or the next case

### Takeaway
None of the three target papers singles out Z/4 or any specific "next" group. The explicit forward-looking statements are: a wish for a simpler Z/3 proof to attack "larger groups, e.g. [SS05, Conjecture 30]" (i.e. K3P, since done); the suggestion to understand K_{1,3} first (Conjecture 4.2: φ(G,3) ≤ |G|) and the constancy conjecture φ(G,n+1) = max(2, φ(G,n)); and — per Michałek's own web page — the general bound φ(G) ≤ |G| is now reported FALSE, with a counterexample for Z/17.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The Z/17 counterexample claim (taken at face value from Michałek's page; I have not seen a paper or the example itself) changes the status of Z/4: the bound φ(Z/4) ≤ 4 can no longer be regarded as an instance of a believed general law. It remains supported only by the [SS] computations for 3 and 4 leaves and by structural analogy with the two solved cases of order ≤ 4. It also means that any Z/4 proof must use something special about small groups — consistent with the fact that both solved proofs are exhaustive small-group case analyses rather than conceptual arguments.
- Which n the Z/17 counterexample lives on, and whether it violates Conjecture 4.2 (K_{1,3}) or only larger n, is not stated on the page; this matters for the "constancy" conjecture 4.3, which is logically independent of the |G| bound.
- If Conjecture 4.3 (constancy in n) were known for Z/4, the [SS] value φ(Z4, 3 leaves) = 4 would immediately give φ(Z/4) = 4. So for Z/4 the real content of the problem is constancy/stabilisation from a computable n onward — exactly what the hand proofs for Z/3 and K3P establish from n = 7.

### Gaps
- No primary write-up of the Z/17 counterexample was located or read; the only source is one paragraph on Michałek's page. Details (n, degree of the offending generator, verification method) are unknown to me.
- I did not search for post-2019 literature on Z/4, Z/5 or cyclic groups (outside my assigned sources), so I cannot say that no partial results exist.

## 7. Candid difficulty estimate for Z/4 by the same method

### Takeaway
My assessment: proving φ(Z/4) = 4 by the Michałek / Michałek–Ventura method is a multi-year, research-monograph-scale case analysis, at least as hard as K3P and plausibly noticeably harder, because Z/4 has fewer symmetries, two inequivalent base strings, a failing exchange lemma, and none of the K3P identities; the skeleton transfers, but essentially every lemma in the technical heart must be reinvented. It is not a routine adaptation. A computer-assisted redesign of the local case analysis looks like the only realistic route.

### Cited Findings (evidence on the cost of the solved cases)
- Z/3: Section 4 is introduced as "the (very technical) proof of Theorem 1.4"; "we failed to find short, easy proofs or conclude simply by deeper and deeper case study"; "we believe that it is impossible to follow this proof without a pen and a sheet of paper." The proof runs about 12 pages (pp. 8–19) with ≈33 numbered lemmas/propositions (4.1–4.33). — [M-Z3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04177)
- K3P: 32-page paper wholly devoted to the proof; n = 5 needed 4ti2 on a 1 TB RAM / 32-core server; n = 6 direct computation "would run for several years ... and a memory of 1Tb RAM would not be sufficient"; ten cases for k = 2. — [MV-K3P](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02584)
- Elapsed effort: Michałek describes K3P as proved "after ten years of fight — maybe even war"; the finiteness paper (2016) announced the plan and the proof appeared in 2017. — [Michałek's conjectures page](https://www.math.uni-konstanz.de/~michalek/conjectures.html), [MV-fin](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07263)
- The general conjecture is reported false (Z/17), so there is no guarantee from "general principles" that the Z/4 statement is even true. — [Michałek's conjectures page](https://www.math.uni-konstanz.de/~michalek/conjectures.html)
- Positive evidence for Z/4: φ(Z4, n) = 4 for the two smallest claw trees computed in 2004 (16 cubics + 6 quartics; 344 quadrics + 256 cubics + 96 quartics). — [SS](https://arxiv.org/abs/q-bio/0402015)

### Inferences (my assessment — not from the papers)
A step-by-step plan for Z/4 modelled on the solved cases, with the point where each step needs new ideas:

1. Base cases n = 3, 4, 5 by 4ti2 (64 and 256 variables for n = 4, 5). Expected feasible: same number of variables as K3P, where n = 5 needed ~1 TB RAM. Needs new ideas: none, but heavy hardware. Risk: if a degree-5 generator shows up at n = 5 the conjecture is dead for Z/4 (cheap and decisive first experiment).
2. n = 6 (1024 variables): direct Markov basis infeasible by the K3P authors' experience. K3P route = Normaliz normality + Hilbert series ⇒ degree ≤ 16, then hand counting of "difference rows" and 4ti2 on codimension-3 faces. For Z/4: requires (a) normality of the 6-leaf Z/4 polytope (unknown to me) and a comparable regularity bound, and (b) a Z/4 version of the main-case structure theory, since the K3P n = 6 argument reuses Lemmas 3.17–3.24. New ideas needed if normality fails; alternatively push the hand proof's threshold or use a symmetry-reduced/fibre-wise computation.
3. n ≥ 7, induction on d ≥ 5 and on Hamming distance k of a closest pair of rows. Group-independent setup.
4. Reduce k ≥ 4 to k ≤ 3: must treat 9 classes of 4-letter strings (K3P: 4). New difficulty: strings like 1111 / 1113-type sub-strings without zero-sum pairs or triples; the K3P trick "a row containing αα can be swapped with 00" has no analogue for the letter 1 (needs 1,3 or 1,1,2 or 1,1,1,1 patterns). Counting-function technique should still work but each string needs its own function and move list.
5. Reduce k = 3 (string 112) to k = 2: analogue of K3P Lemmas 3.7–3.11 + Prop. 3.12 (about 3 pages there).
6. k = 2: two roots, (1,3) and (2,2), each with a K3P-style enumeration of the second and third rows in both tables under a symmetry group of order only 2·2 (negation, column transposition) plus flow translation. Expect several times the K3P count of 10 cases. This is where degree-4 moves will be indispensable and where the bulk of new invention lies: one needs Z/4 replacements for the Difference Lemma and Standard Lemma, and a new treatment of rows congruent mod 2 (the failure locus of Lemma 3.1).
7. Final step in each main case: make all rows agree with a template on two columns (or kill "bad pairs") and merge columns. Group-independent once the structure theory of step 6 exists; the threshold n ≥ 7 may have to increase because Z/4 exchange lemmas need 3 disagreements with an odd one rather than 2.

Reasons it is harder than K3P despite |G| = 4 in both:
- Symmetry: |Aut| = 2 versus 6; non-transitive on nonzero elements.
- Arithmetic: no x + x = 0 for x = 1, 3; no α+β+γ = 0; Davenport constant 4 instead of 3 (longer minimal zero-sum patterns, hence longer forced exchanges).
- A characteristic subgroup {0,2} creates a parity obstruction to exchanges that cannot be normalised away.
- Motivation/verification: both solved proofs are unformalised hand case analyses that their own author calls nearly unreadable; a Z/4 proof several times longer would be hard to referee. Realistically it should be organised so that the local case analysis (finite tables on ≤ 5–6 special columns and ≤ 4–5 special rows, entries in Z/4, "exists a move of degree ≤ 4 that decreases the potential or else a counting contradiction") is discharged by computer search, with humans supplying only the potential function and the merge step.

Reasons for cautious optimism:
- The skeleton (three inductions + column merging) is proven technology and group-independent.
- The quotient to Z/2 and the subgroup {0,2} give a two-level structure (a Z/2-flow of "parities" plus Z/2-valued corrections) that might allow a more conceptual argument combining the quadratic Z/2 theorem with K3P-style arguments; no source suggests this, it is speculation.
- A much easier intermediate target exists: the scheme-theoretic statement for Z/4 à la [DB] (six flow types instead of three), and the K_{1,3}-type statements, both of which would be publishable partial results and would stress-test the conjecture for Z/4.

Bottom line: difficulty roughly "K3P × (2 to 4)" in case volume with at least three genuinely new lemmas required (exchange lemma replacing Lemma 3.1; replacements for the Difference/Standard Lemmas; a treatment of the n = 6 base case), and with the additional risk — after the reported Z/17 counterexample — that the statement might fail for some n ≥ 5, which should be probed computationally (n = 5) before investing in a proof.

### Gaps
- The multiplier "2 to 4" is a judgement from counting strings and symmetries, not a measured quantity.
- I have not attempted any of the Z/4 lemmas; the claim that counting-function arguments "should still work" for the nine 4-letter strings is untested.
- Feasibility of the n = 5 Z/4 Markov basis computation is inferred from the K3P n = 5 run (same number of variables); actual cost depends on the ideal and could differ substantially.
